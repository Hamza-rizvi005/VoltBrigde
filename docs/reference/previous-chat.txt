There are a few important things I want you to understand before we go further.

1. Your electricity-sharing idea has a serious obstacle
Your assumption is that if House A produces 100 units and uses 50, the remaining 50 units can simply be transferred to House B.

Physically, electricity can flow through a connected grid, but the commercial and regulatory right to sell electricity to another household is a separate matter.

As of October 2026, K-Electric's published FAQs on Pakistan's 2026 Prosumer Regulations explicitly state that electricity generated by a prosumer can be shared with its utility, not sold directly to other customers. New installations also fall under net billing rather than the old net-metering arrangement.

That doesn't mean you should abandon the idea. It means we need to investigate whether a compliant arrangement involving an authorized utility, licensed supplier, or another permitted structure could work.

2. Electricity units aren't the same as guaranteed electricity availability
Imagine House A generates 50 surplus units during sunny afternoons, but House B needs electricity at night.

Those 50 units don't automatically become 50 units of nighttime electricity for House B. The arrangement would need to account for when electricity is generated, how it is delivered, metering, grid losses, and payment.

And if House B is experiencing load shedding, merely matching it with a solar owner won't guarantee that its lights will turn on. The physical delivery arrangement matters.

3. Your land-rental idea could be a separate business
Your example is a form of land brokerage or site aggregation.

You find suitable unused land, verify the owner's authority to lease it, connect the owner with a business that needs the site, and earn a disclosed brokerage fee or agreed commission.

But there is another question: does the bank need land to install solar panels, or would it be better served by leasing a suitable rooftop, using another location under a permitted power-purchase arrangement, or buying power from a solar developer?

We shouldn't assume a bank would rent an empty plot just because it lacks roof space. The economics, distance from the load, transmission arrangements, security, and lease terms all matter.
